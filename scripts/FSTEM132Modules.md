Here is the fully consolidated layout of your editorial guidelines. The structure has been optimized to read like an operational manual—modular, uncompromising, and built for tactical execution. Operational Editorial Guidelines: Total Domain Warfare I. Strategic Intent: Tone & Audience Target Reader: Write for an educated, 20-year-old reader. Be direct and professional. Never condescend. The Grant Standard: The style must echo Ulysses S. Grant. Prioritize commanding authority, objectivity, and absolute clarity without decorative flourish. Strip Meta-Commentary: Let the evidence do the heavy lifting. Do not step out of the narrative to comment on the text itself. ❌ Example: "The point for the framework is structural and certain." ✅ Correction: [Delete. State the structural point directly.] II. Tactical Execution: Syntax & Word Choice Command the Active Voice: Search ruthlessly for "is," "was," "are," and "by the." Recast to position the actor at the front of the sentence. Force Multipliers (Verbs > Adverbs): Prefer one powerful, specific verb to a weak verb propped up by an adverb. ❌ Example: "quickly ran" → ✅ Correction: "bolted" ❌ Example: "very important" → ✅ Correction: "critical" or "essential" Eradicate "It is" and "There are" Openers: These create instant passivity and delay the subject. ❌ Example: "There is a return-based measure too." ✅ Correction: "A return-based measure also exists." Subject-Verb Proximity: Keep the primary actor and the action adjacent. Do not force the reader to carry heavy context across long, modifying clauses. ❌ Example: "The SEC, in Press Release 2020-319 and an accompanying complaint, announced a penalty." ✅ Correction: "The SEC announced a penalty in Press Release 2020-319." III. The Framework: Structural Punctuation The "One Shot" Rule for Sentences: If a sentence requires more than two commas to function, break it into two distinct sentences. Prioritize punch over elegance. Eliminate Em-Dashes (—): Replace them with either shorter, separate sentences or stronger logical connectors (and, but, because, so, yet). ❌ Example: "The plan failed—we regrouped." ✅ Correction: "The plan failed. We regrouped." OR "The plan failed, so we regrouped." IV. Noise Reduction: Eradicating AI Patterns No Enumeration Slop: Avoid rhythmic, repetitive contrasts. Replace them with a direct assertion or a maximum of one to two tight contrasts. ❌ Example: "It is not a strategy. It is not a tactic. It is not a process. It is a mindset." ✅ Correction: "It is a mindset, not a tactic." Every Word Earns Its Place: Cut filler. If a word does not advance the argument or clarify the data, delete it. V. Mission Alignment: Content Strategy Information Style: Deploy exposition, raw data, and structural clarity when the objective is to build the reader's baseline understanding. Persuasion Style: Deploy argument, documented evidence, and resonance when the objective is to drive home the thesis. Do Not Force Fit: Match the style to the objective. Do not force persuasion where plain information serves the reader better.
